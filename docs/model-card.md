# Model card: voluntary attrition risk

Generated by `python -m pipeline.build` for data ending October 2026. Model `lgbm-202610-s7`.

## What it is for

A ranked list of employees who are more likely than their colleagues to resign in the next
six months, with the factors behind each score, so an HR business partner can start a
conversation about pay, workload or career early. It is **not** for decisions about
individuals: not for performance ratings, promotion, discipline or termination.

## Data

Synthetic. A month-by-month simulation of a fictional insurer (see `simulation.md`), so
the true causes of attrition are known and the model can be checked against them.
Rows are employee-months; the label is a voluntary exit in the following 6 months.

| Split | Months | Employee-months |
|---|---|---|
| train | 2024-02-01 to 2025-04-01 | 10,763 |
| valid | 2025-05-01 to 2025-10-01 | 4,377 |
| test | 2025-11-01 to 2026-04-01 | 4,448 |
| score | 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-01 | 743 |

Features: `tenure_months`, `grade_rank`, `pay_ratio`, `months_since_promotion`, `overtime_3m`, `rating`, `manager_changed_6m`, `on_pkwt`, `pay_change_12m`, `department`, `location`.
Gender and age are deliberately **not** features; they are used only in the audit below.

## Results on the test months

- Base rate: 7.4% of employee-months are followed by a voluntary exit within six months.
- ROC-AUC 0.619, PR-AUC 0.099 (a random ranking would score PR-AUC ≈ 0.074).
- Brier score 0.0691 before calibration, 0.0688 after (Platt scaling; isotonic regression was tried and made it worse).
- Calibrated probabilities average 6.0% on the test months against 7.4% observed. The calibration was fitted on the validation months, when 5.8% resigned, so the levels run low when the rate rises. The ranking, which is what the risk list uses, is unaffected; recalibrate on recent months before reading the probabilities as levels.
- **Ceiling:** ranking the same rows by the true planted formula (which no real dataset would have, and without the unobserved personal factor) scores ROC-AUC 0.641 and PR-AUC 0.132. Most of the distance to a perfect score is noise in who decides to leave, not something better modelling could recover.
- Scoring a single month (2025-11-01, 743 employees, 47 of whom resigned within six months): the top 10% (74 people) contained 19% of those leavers, a lift of 1.92× over picking at random.

Equal flag rates are not the goal when underlying rates differ; the table shows whether flags
track observed outcomes in each group. Large gaps between *observed rate* and *mean predicted*
for a group would mean the model is miscalibrated for that group, which is what to watch.

## Limitations

- Trained on simulated data with a known, simple structure. Real attrition has causes no HRIS records.
- Six months of horizon and monthly snapshots; a resignation decided in the last week of a month
  is invisible until the next snapshot.
- Scores are relative within the scored month (top 10% = high, next 20% = elevated).
- Access: individual scores only for HR admins and HRBPs in scope; leaders see aggregates.
